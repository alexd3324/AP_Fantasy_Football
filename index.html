Expected wins
Each week a team's score is compared with every other team's score, as if it played all of them. Beating 8 of 11 teams counts as 0.73 of a win. The weekly values add up over the season.
Wins above expected
Actual wins minus expected wins. Positive means a team has won more than its scoring deserves. Negative means it has been unlucky.
Schedule luck
The league's average score minus the average score of the opponents a team actually faced, in points per game. Positive means an easier schedule than average.

ⓘ Projection methodology & limitations

3,000 simulated regular seasons using completed matchup scores, the actual remaining Sleeper schedule, and the same record-based berths plus points wildcard used by playoff forecasts. Weekly scoring is sampled from a shrunk normal distribution.

Max PF estimate: Sleeper does not provide future optimal lineups. We estimate future Max PF from simulated weekly scores multiplied by each franchise’s current season Max PF / actual PF ratio, shrunk toward the league average. This is an approximation, not an official future Max PF forecast.

Projected Draft Slot: every team receives a numerical average across all simulated seasons. Non-playoff slots use reverse Max PF. For playoff qualifiers, slots 7–12 are provisional seed-based placeholders (best regular-season seed assigned the latest pick), NOT simulated playoff outcomes or official draft order. The chart marks these playoff-slot estimates with an asterisk. Top 6 is the probability of missing the playoffs. Actual playoff draft slots depend on postseason results.

ⓘ How draft capital is calculated

Capital score weights rounds 1–4 at 100, 45, 20 and 8 points, discounted for later years. Only the upcoming draft uses projected pick slots; future years are valued by round, without speculative positions.

Upcoming draft: non-playoff picks are projected from reverse Max PF. Playoff slots require final bracket placement. Future drafts show the round and original franchise, not speculative pick numbers.

How these rankings are calculated

1 · Value each selection: For QB, RB, WR and TE, fantasy production percentile is converted to a nonlinear 0–100 production score and current Stats Guy Fantasy value to a separate 0–100 market score. With a market quote, the player score is 50% production and 50% market. Without a quote, the clearly labeled production-only score is used; a missing quote is not assumed to be zero. Players below the 50th production percentile get no production credit.

2 · Include IDP fairly: Defensive players use the same position-relative production scoring, then receive a 0.40 player-value multiplier. This is a disclosed assumption, not an IDP market price.

3 · Recalibrate pick cost: Within each historical draft, observed player scores form the reference distribution. Earlier slots carry a smoothly declining cost curve, blended with the observed class outcomes; late-round costs are never artificially flattened. Crucially, expected values and actual values now use the same scale. A complete draft therefore has approximately zero total surplus across all managers.

4 · Rank franchises: Each pick contributes player value minus calibrated pick expectation. Best Draft Class adds these differences; Best Talent Evaluator averages with four neutral picks of sample-size shrinkage. Click any franchise to audit its contributions.

Read carefully: Scores are relative to the other selections in the same draft, not a claim that a manager generated an absolute financial gain. The within-class benchmark is descriptive, not a historical success-probability model. Missing market values, incomplete historical scoring, and recent rookies make some comparisons provisional. Filtering positions preserves the original all-position baseline.
